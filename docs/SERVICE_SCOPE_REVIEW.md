## Regulatory sources checked

- [Consumer Affairs Victoria: becoming a registered building practitioner](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/licensing-and-registration/builders-and-tradespeople/checklists/becoming-a-registered-building-practitioner). Registration is generally required for domestic building work exceeding **$10,000 including labour and materials**, including combinations of work. Permit-requiring work and listed restricted activities require registration regardless of value. A low price alone does not establish an exemption.
- [Building and Plumbing Commission: domestic building work and classes](https://www.vba.vic.gov.au/registration-and-licensing/building-practitioner-registration/domestic-builder/what-is-domestic-building-work). Carpentry, joinery, external cladding, doors/windows and attached fixtures can fall within registered building work. “Nonstructural” is not a blanket exemption.
- [SA Government: building work contractor's licence](https://www.sa.gov.au/topics/business-and-trade/licensing/building-and-trades/building-work-contractor-s-licence). Businesses, including subcontractors, carrying out or arranging building work need the relevant contractor licence. Sole traders ordinarily also need a registered supervisor.
- [Consumer and Business Services SA: building licence FAQs](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au/documents/occlicensing/Building-licence-faqs.pdf). Working for another licensed builder as an independent subcontractor still requires licensing; employees are treated differently.
- [Legal Services Commission SA: minor domestic building work](https://lsc.sa.gov.au/handbook/ch23s11s04.php). The current minor-contract threshold is **less than $20,000**, increased from $12,000 on 10 November 2025. This changes contract/insurance requirements; it is **not an exemption from trade licensing**.

Sources accessed 11 November 2026. Thresholds are recorded here for owner review, not marketed as an allowance to do unregistered work. Recheck current rules when quoting, including commercial work and work outside Victoria.

Consumer Affairs Victoria also announces [domestic building contract law changes taking effect by 1 December 2026](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/latest-news/new-domestic-building-contract-laws-passed-in-victoria). Recheck commencement and current requirements for later quotations. No website price, payment schedule or licence exemption is based on those forthcoming changes.
